TRust JS feedback schema (SCHEMA_VERSION 1)

What generated and interpreted code record about the operations they run, for the speculative
tier (the plan's Phase 3) to read. crates/trust-js/src/feedback.rs holds the encodings; this file
is their reference. The numeric encodings below are append-only within a schema version: a value
may be added after the last one of its type, never renumbered or reused. Recorded state only ever
grows (an observed class is never forgotten), so a reader may trust anything a record holds and
must treat an empty record as "not observed", never as "impossible".

1. Where feedback lives today

Production feedback is the baseline's own caches; nothing extra is recorded on a hit.
- Named property reads and stores: the property inline caches (IcState ways: receiver shape,
  holder shape, depth, slot location; bytecode.rs), filled on the miss path.
- Calls and constructions: CallSite and CallIc (callee identity, function literal, chunk, realm,
  epoch; the megamorphic state, W3 item 7), ConstructSite (the prototype's shape and slot).
- Elements: the word block's kind bits (word_block.rs: NO_HOLES, NUMBERS, INT32, FROZEN, COW,
  SEALED), read live by the element templates.
- Allocations: a literal site's image (W2e: bytecode.rs ObjectImage), a constructor's learned
  capacity and class (Chunk::note_instance, W2d step 3).
The detailed vector below (section 3) is a diagnostic, never an acceptance setting: it routes the
operations it observes through helpers, so it does not observe optimized execution.

2. Encodings

OperationKind (u8): the source-level operation a site represents.
  1 NamedLoad, 2 NamedStore, 3 ElementLoad, 4 ElementStore, 5 Call, 6 Construct, 7 Arithmetic,
  8 Branch, 9 Loop, 10 Allocation.
ObservationKind (u8): what a slot's payload means.
  1 ValueClass, 2 ReceiverLayout, 3 HolderLayout, 4 ElementAccess, 5 CallTarget, 6 BranchCount,
  7 Allocation, 8 PropertyAccess.
ObservationRole (u8): a slot's position in its site.
  1 Operand0, 2 Operand1, 3 Result, 4 Receiver, 5 Holder, 6 Access, 7 Target, 8 Outcome.
ObservationState (u8): a slot's abstract state.
  0 Uninitialized, 1 Monomorphic, 2 Polymorphic, 3 Absent, 4 Generic.
ValueClass (u8; a set is a bitmask with bit (class - 1)): ECMAScript's types, with Number split
  into an optimizer refinement that never changes the operation's Number semantics.
  1 Undefined, 2 Null, 3 Boolean, 4 NumberInt32 (an int32 value without -0), 5 NumberDouble,
  6 String, 7 BigInt, 8 Symbol, 9 Object.
PropertyOutcome (u8): a named access's result family, in the high nibble of the observation's
  flags; the low nibble's bits 0-2 hold the prototype depth (PROPERTY_DEPTH_MASK 0x07) and bit 3
  that an array key was checked (PROPERTY_ARRAY_KEY_CHECK 0x08).
  1 Data, 2 Absent, 3 Created, 4 Accessor, 5 Exotic, 6 Rejected.
ElementAccess payload: three bit sets, each with bit (value - 1) at its shift.
  Receiver (shift 0): 1 Ordinary, 2 Array, 3 TypedArray, 4 String, 5 Primitive, 6 Exotic.
  Key (shift 8): 1 Index, 2 CanonicalNumeric, 3 String, 4 Symbol.
  Outcome (shift 16): 1 OwnData, 2 Hole, 3 Prototype, 4 Absent, 5 Accessor, 6 Exotic, 7 Created,
  8 Rejected.
CallTarget payload: three bit sets.
  Target (shift 0): 1 UserFunction, 2 NativeFunction, 3 BoundFunction, 4 Proxy,
  5 WrappedFunction, 6 CallableObject, 7 NonCallable.
  Arity (shift 8): 1 Zero, 2 One, 3 Few, 4 Many (the expanded list's length for spread calls).
  Environment (shift 16): 1 None, 2 Captured, 3 Dynamic, 4 Unknown.
Allocation payload: two bit sets.
  Object (shift 0): 1 Function, 2 RegExp, 3 Array, 4 Object.
  Size (shift 8): 1 Zero, 2 Small, 3 Medium, 4 Large (array elements, object properties or a
  RegExp's source bytes, never allocator metadata).

An observation word (u64): bits 0-7 the ObservationState, 8-15 the flags, 16-47 the payload.
Exact indices, strings, addresses, shape numbers and Value encodings are never recorded in an
observation: they are workload facts or representation details, which belong to the versioned
runtime adapters (the caches of section 1).

3. The detailed vector (TRUST_JS_FEEDBACK_PROFILE=1, a diagnostic)

A chunk's FeedbackLayout lists its sites (SiteDescriptor: bytecode pc, first slot, operation,
slot count) and their slots (SlotDescriptor: kind and role); its FeedbackVector holds one
observation word a slot, merged monotonically. Its profile envelope (serialize_profile), little
endian, 48 bytes of header then one word a slot:
  0   8 bytes  magic "TRUST_JS"
  8   u16      format version (1)
  10  u16      schema version (SCHEMA_VERSION, 1)
  12  u8       scope (1: vector-local, meaningful only to the vector that wrote it)
  13  3 bytes  zero
  16  u64      the process's profile session
  24  u64      the vector's id
  32  u64      the layout's stable hash
  40  u32      site count
  44  u32      slot count
A merge (merge_serialized_profile) validates all of it and refuses a profile from another
session, vector or layout.

4. Coverage and precision (W4's standing metrics)

Coverage: the share of the speculation kinds' executed operations (named and element loads and
stores, calls, constructions, arithmetic, unary, update and comparison operations) whose site the
speculative tier could compile without an exit for missing feedback (V8's sense of usable
feedback; megamorphic and generic count as usable). Precision: the share whose feedback names
exactly the coarse classes the operation saw. W4's census (the plan's "W4: feedback for the
speculative tier") measured both.

The census tool (W4 step 2; crates/trust-js/src/feedback_census.rs, compiled only with the
architecture-diagnostics feature, so ordinary builds pay nothing) measures them on any program:
TRUST_JS_FEEDBACK_CENSUS=<path> writes, at the shell's exit, the operations the VM's loop
dispatched by chunk and pc and each site's production feedback; scripts/feedback-census.py joins
a --tier=bytecode run (whose dispatches are every compiled-code execution) with a --tier=jit
run (whose snapshot is the production feedback) and prints both shares by kind (DIAGNOSTICS.md).
Its file's header names the records the engine writes (`records=`), whose readings the script
applies. A site counts as usable when:
- named load or store: a filled way of its property cache, or its generic mark (step 5);
- call: a filled way or the megamorphic state of its CallSite (spread and argument-array calls
  have no cache);
- construct: its ConstructSite's way;
- allocation: its image (object and array literals, RegExp literals), or always for closures and
  empty literals, which need no per-site state;
- instanceof: its cache's way; typeof and `in`: always (the templates lower them generically);
- arithmetic, unary, update and comparison operations: always, once the value record exists
  (step 3): its record and in-line set are the feedback, and equality is lowered by tag;
- element loads and stores: always, once the element record exists (step 4): its record and the
  in-line set's two-way dispatch, as the templates lower it;
- the records' other kinds, as the steps below add them.
Precision counts the caches' sites as coverage does (a generic named site is precise: it is
megamorphic or uncacheable, exactly what the speculative tier lowers generically), a value
operation when its record read by
rule U (section 5.1) names exactly the coarse classes the bytecode run's record saw (the VM records
every execution there), an element access when its record read by rule R (section 5.2) names
exactly the receivers and keys that record saw, and typeof, `in` and equality, which record
nothing, as not precise.
Branches, loops, `!` (ToBoolean) and names are counted but left out: no exemplar records branch
directions or ToBoolean, and names mostly have static addresses. Equality has no record by design
(W4 decision 3), so the script prints a second total without it.

5. The always-on records (W4; each step adds its record here)

5.1 The value record (step 3). A chunk's feedback vector holds one u32 a bytecode pc, in an array
it makes at the chunk's first record (FeedbackVector::record), so a chunk that never records holds
none. An arithmetic, unary, update or relational-comparison operation's word holds three
ValueClass sets: operand 0 in bits 0-8, operand 1 in bits 9-17 (RECORD_OPERAND1_SHIFT), the result
in bits 18-26 (RECORD_RESULT_SHIFT); a set has bit (class - 1). A record only grows: the writer
ORs the classes in and stores only when the word changes.

Who writes it:
- The VM, on every execution of these operations (its arithmetic, unary, update and comparison
  paths, and the string accumulation's fused concatenation).
- Generated code, only where its in-line path missed: jit_exec's operations, String + String's
  helper (jit_add_strings), the string accumulation, the update helpers. A miss records only
  values outside its operation's in-line set, so a backend that runs an operation out of line
  (x86-64's Add, Sub, Mul, Div, `%`, shifts, Neg, updates) records what ARM64's in-line path would.
- Nothing on an in-line path, and nothing for equality (==, !=, ===, !==): the speculative tier
  types equality from its operands, as JSC does (the plan's W4 decision 3).

The in-line sets, by operation (operand 0, operand 1, result):
  Add, Sub, Mul, Div, UShr, `**` (GenBin)        Number, Number, Number
  BitAnd, BitOr, BitXor, Shl, Shr                Number, Number, Number (an int32)
  % (Mod)                                       int32, int32, int32 (not -0)
  Neg, Plus, BitNot; the updates (++, --:
    UpdateLocal, UpdateCap, UpdateName,
    UpdateNameCached, UpdateConst, UpdatePrivate,
    SuperUpdate, UpdateProp, UpdateElem)         Number, -, Number
  Lt, Gt, Le, Ge                                Number, Number, -
Number is NumberInt32 | NumberDouble. ARM64's bit operations also take Booleans in line without a
record; their in-line set stays Number, so a Boolean there reads as a miss the speculative tier
did not see (it exits, and its exit profile learns it).

Reading it (rule U): the classes an operation saw are its record together with its in-line set,
for each role. That is complete: every class outside the in-line set was recorded on a miss or by
the VM (W4's census: 99.8-100% of arithmetic executions). It is exact at the coarse grain (Number
not split) where the in-line set's classes were seen or the record holds them. The int32/double
split inside the in-line set is known only where the VM or a miss recorded it: a hint for
speculation guarded by exits, not a proof.

5.2 The element record (step 4). An element load's or store's word (GetElem, GetElemLocal,
GetMethodElem, SetElem, SetElemDrop, SetElemLocal, SetElemLocalDrop; UpdateElem's word is the
value record's) holds three bit sets, a byte each (feedback.rs ELEM_*), ORed in like the value
record's:
  Receiver (bits 0-6), by its kind's tag: 0 an Array, 1 a typed array, 2 another ordinary kind
    (ObjectKind::is_ordinary: plain objects, functions, a Map's or Set's data; a side-table
    object such as a module namespace counts here, and generated code tells it by ic_plain),
    3 an arguments object, 4 any other object (wrappers, errors, iterators, proxies), 5 a String,
    6 any other primitive (undefined and null included).
  Key (bits 8-12), before ToPropertyKey: 8 a Number that is an array index (an integer in
    0..2^32-1, -0 included), 9 another Number, 10 a String, 11 a Symbol, 12 anything else.
  An Array's facts (bits 16-20), from its element words' kind bits (word_block.rs): 16 a hole
    (NO_HOLES clear), 17 a non-Number (NUMBERS clear), 18 a non-int32 (INT32 clear), 19 frozen
    or a shared literal's block (FROZEN or COW), 20 an index key at or past the used words (a
    read out of bounds, a store that appends or leaves a gap).

Who writes it:
- The VM, on every element access, before it runs it.
- The element helpers (H_GET_ELEM's, H_SET_ELEM's, jit_exec's element operations, the elided and
  lazy arguments' reads), only for an access outside the in-line set: get_computed_element and
  set_computed_element record where fast_get_elem, fast_set_elem and ordinary_set_existing_index
  decline, for a String key, and for a String receiver. x86-64, whose element accesses all take
  these helpers, records what ARM64's in-line paths would.
- Nothing on an in-line path.

The in-line sets (receiver, key):
  loads    an own data element of an Array or another ordinary object, or a typed array's
           element (not BigInt content), at an array index: fast_get_elem's set, which holds
           ARM64's templates' (an Array's or an ordinary object's words, a typed array's view)
  stores   an own writable data element of an Array or another ordinary object, an Array's
           append (extensible, its prototype Array.prototype with the protector intact), an
           existing writable data property at an index, or a typed array's element with a value
           of its content type, at an array index: fast_set_elem's and
           ordinary_set_existing_index's set
In the record's terms: an Array, typed-array or ordinary receiver (bits 0-2) with an index key
(bit 8).

Reading it: rule U, the receivers and keys an access saw are its record's together with the
in-line set's; that is complete. Rule R, the record alone where it holds a receiver or key and
the in-line set's where it holds none, is what the census's precision measures: it names them
exactly where the VM's iteration or a miss also saw the in-line hits' receivers, and an empty
record never tells an Array from a typed array or an ordinary object (the plan's design 2: P3
keeps the baseline's dispatch there). An Array's facts are hints, as the value record's int32
is: an in-line hit records none, so a fact is known only where the VM or a miss saw it, and P3
reads the kind bits at run time, as the templates do.

5.3 The named sites' generic mark (step 5). A named load's or store's property cache (its four
IcState ways) carries one site-level bit: bit 7 of way 0's mid_ok (bytecode.rs IC_MID_GENERIC),
which no probe reads (the templates test mid_ok's bits 0-3 one by one). The fill path sets it,
and only it:
- a refused fill: a read's uncacheable receiver or chain (a level ic_plain refuses: a proxy, a
  typed array, a side-table object), a store's receiver its cache cannot hold (an Array or other
  exotic, a proxy, a side-table object), a state whose shapes are uncacheable;
- a fill that evicts a live way: a fifth receiver shape (V8's megamorphic transition past its
  four maps; CallSite's megamorphic state counts its own evictions, W3 item 7).
Interp::ic_insert carries the mark into each new way 0; nothing clears it. Reading it: a marked
site is generic, which the speculative tier lowers to the stub cache's probe or the generic
[[Get]] and [[Set]], its ways a fast path, and never an exit for missing feedback. An unmarked
site's filled ways are every receiver shape it saw, up to four, but for fills refused for the
property (a non-writable or index-named one, a slot past the cache's reach, a deeper chain),
which leave no mark. The census's state word reports the mark in bit 7 (`generic` in its header's
records).

5.4 The read API (step 6). crate::feedback::read(chunk, pc) answers one view of the operation at
a baseline pc (feedback_read.rs; the chunk's side in bytecode_feedback_read.rs), allocating and
interning nothing: its shapes are the process's shape ids, its targets the process's cells.
- Values (arithmetic, unary, update and relational operations, UpdateElem's too): the three
  ValueClass sets by rule U, and the record alone, whose int32/double split is a hint.
- Element (element loads and stores): the receivers and keys by rule U, and the record alone with
  an Array's facts, hints.
- Named (named loads and stores): each filled way, most recent first (receiver and holder shapes,
  depth, slot, slot location, kind: data, key-checked, accessor, absent or creation), and the
  generic mark; NamedUpdate (UpdateProp) adds the value record's view.
- Call (Call, CallWithThis, CallSpread, CallSpreadThis): the state (empty, monomorphic,
  polymorphic, megamorphic) and each way's target: the callee's cell, its function literal (the
  FeedbackCell's role: closures of one literal share it), a native's builtin id and entry, the
  callee's realm, and whether the entry was filled in the current call-cache epoch.
- Construct (New): its target and the instances' prototype's shape.
- Allocation: the kind (object, array, RegExp, closure) and whether the image and the template (an
  object literal's template, an array literal's shared block, a RegExp literal's compiled
  pattern) exist.
- None: the rest, equality (no record by design), typeof and `in` among them.
Exact: the ways and targets as read, each a proof that generated code re-validates (a shape is
never reused; a way may name a shape no live object has any more), and an epoch-current call
target. Hints: the int32/double split and an Array's facts. Stale: a call target of an older
epoch, whose chunk and code may have been replaced (its callee and literal stay). The
constructor's capacity class stays in the callee's chunk (Chunk::instance_class); the
allocation sites' survival record went to Phase 3 with W4's decision 2. The detailed vector
(section 3) and its envelope stay diagnostics.

5.5 The pc map of inlined and recompiled code (step 7). A transformed chunk (a second-stage
compile, its hot callees spliced in) maps each of its value, element and named operations to the
baseline site it was compiled from (feedback.rs RecordMap): an operation of the function's own
body to the function's baseline chunk, a splice's to its callee's baseline chunk. Both compiles
read the same AST, the function's own body around its splices and each splice from its callee's
body, so within one origin (the function's own body, or one splice, told apart by its position
in the caller) the k-th operation of a kind is the k-th of that kind in the origin's baseline
chunk; a kind whose count differs between the two stays unmapped in that origin. (Source
positions would not do: the compiler attributes them to the nodes that can throw a located
error, calls and property accesses, not to arithmetic.) One u32 a pc (the target's index in bits
24-31, its pc in 0-23), and the targets, which the map keeps alive. The transformed chunk's
vector (FeedbackVector::transformed) sends its always-on records and its detailed observations
through the map into the baseline sites (its own words stay empty), so a callee's sites see what
its inlined copies saw; an operation the map cannot place records nothing. Its caches stay its
own (they are its speed): crate::feedback::read_merged adds a transformed chunk's ways of a site
to the baseline site's named view, a union past four ways being generic. The speculative tier's
own exits (Phase 3) record by baseline pc through the same map, as JSC's CodeOrigin and
FrequentExitSite do.

5.6 Store writability, Pilot C (step 9). A store's or an update's site (SetProp, SetPropDrop,
SetPropThisDrop, SetPropLocalDrop, AppendProp, UpdateProp: bytecode.rs writes_property) holds data
ways for writable data properties only: Interp::try_ic_set fills and promotes nothing else; an
update's read (get_update_property) fills only an own writable data property and promotes
nothing from the stub cache, which reads share; a recompilation's seeds from a read site's ways
never stay in a store's or an update's site (clear_read_seeded_stores). Shapes key the
properties' attributes (W2d step 2), so a way's matching shape proves its property still a
writable data property: ARM64's SetProp and UpdateProp templates and the loop chains' property
stores (whose states are store sites' ways) test no attribute byte any more
(guard_slot_writable_data: three instructions where the layout has no attribute bytes, seven
where it has). The read API reports it (NamedView::writable): a store's or an update's site's
Data ways are writable data properties. x86-64 keeps its shared live checks.
