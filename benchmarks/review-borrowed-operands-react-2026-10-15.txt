Borrowed operands: native React SSR comparison, 2026-10-15
==========================================================

Result and scope
----------------
The same native x86-64 release CLI, with only borrowed operands toggled from 0 to
1, used 2.65% less renderer-and-equality time on the fixed React 19.2.7 SSR
workload. The preregistered empirical 95% paired-bootstrap interval is 1.31% to
3.29% less time. Sampled process peak RSS increased 2.44%, and dashboard-static
rendering took 1.76% longer. These costs remain part of the result.

This measures a separate frozen 826-file runtime-probe snapshot based on f09aac63,
including selected native PCs, borrowed windows and lazy owner-helper addresses.
It is not a measurement of later main or logical-activation compositions. The
optimizing-jit feature, hot runtime selection and borrowed operands remain opt-in;
no default, hard IR/block cap or configured hotness threshold changed. This is an
authored SSR comparison, not browser/Speedometer or Node/Ladybird performance.

Protocol and correctness
------------------------
Five baseline variance processes preceded seven fixed alternating pairs:
B/C, C/B, B/C, C/B, B/C, C/B, B/C. Every fresh process ran the unchanged six cases
128 times, with no additional warmup, calls, drain, selected cases or thresholds.
The fixed 19 processes completed 14,592 renders. Every render checked complete
HTML equality internally; independent replay also checked all 228 retained first
and last complete UTF-8 bodies and their hashes against the pinned oracle.
Distinct bodies for all 14,592 renders are not independently logged.

Phase0 primary totals were 15799, 15817, 15852, 15752, 15676 ms: relative MAD
0.2975%, range/median 1.1140%, within the preregistered 1% and 6% limits. After
all pairs, an arm's primary sample beyond max(6*MAD, 6% of arm median) would have
excluded the entire cohort. None did. No sample was deleted, replaced or pooled.
The bootstrap uses random.Random(20261015), 50,000 resamples of seven complete
pair indices with replacement, median candidate/baseline ratio for each draw,
and linear q*(N-1) percentiles at 2.5 and 97.5. It ran only after measurement and
raw evidence sealing. Independent review reproduced the arrays, draw hash,
intervals, correctness, resource calculations and policy flags.

Renderer CPU was 5, controller CPU 4; CPU 1 is CPU 5's physical sibling and had
zero busy ticks in retained per-process samples. No governor was changed: the
recorded governor was powersave with a 3,500,000 kHz maximum. The dedicated native
host was Zombie, Linux 7.2.9-2-cachyos, x86_64. Measurement ran from
2026-10-15 03:49:55 to 03:55:01 UTC. The per-process watchdog was 600 seconds and
the prospective native whole-cohort watchdog 1200 seconds.

The sampled 0.2-second comm/executable guard and between-process checks found no
foreign worker. They cannot prove global idleness or exclude unknown/sub-200ms
activity. Seven expected children briefly lacked /proc/exe while exiting. Each
observation matched the actual unreaped child PID/start and stable parent; the
controller required exact-PID/UID terminal waitid(WEXITED|WNOHANG|WNOWAIT) proof
before wait4. State alone never exempted a worker. All 19 processes exited 0,
passed affinity/output/pin/oracle checks and left no owned group survivors or
cleanup errors. No other SSH command ran during the cohort.

Retained failures and interpretation limits
------------------------------------------
Two earlier Crystal cohorts and the first native cohort remain wholly excluded.
Crystal V1 stopped on its own exited child's unavailable exe; Crystal V2 stopped
on an unattributed Cargo worker. Native V1 stopped on its own unreaped child in
state R with unavailable exe (11 completed valid processes). None supplied partial
pair ratios or bootstrap samples. Their reports and cleanup records were retained.
The reviewed guard successor passed 48 source/pure/real-child checks before this
new prospective cohort; no workload was replayed to fill a missing sample.

Normal-policy compilation occurs within applicable workload clocks, but isolated
compilation cost and repayment were not measured. There is no separate startup,
pause/tail-latency or post-GC retained-memory result. No fully uninstrumented
process claim is made: the cold final coverage export remains. This evidence
supports only the exact authored SSR comparison. It neither enables the optimizer
or borrowing by default nor establishes browser, whole-engine or Node/Ladybird
performance, a completed optimizing tier or current-main performance acceptance.
