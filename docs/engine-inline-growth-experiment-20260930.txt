Large caller inline growth — blocked by semantics, parked 30 September 2026
==========================================================================

The existing whole-body source-op budget leaves callers above 320 operations
with no inline allowance. The solver's large lexer is one such target; its
small character-advance helper cannot be spliced under that policy. The
prototype grants a bounded minimum remaining budget of 64 source operations
to callers up to 4096 ops, retaining existing context, size, environment,
identity and nesting guards. Explicit diagnostic budgets retain their old
meaning. This is an admission experiment, not a measured speedup.

The native publication regression passes and demonstrates an inline guard
in a caller above the old limit, with replacement, getter effects, throws
and collection checked across interpreter, bytecode and template JIT.
All 29 selected inlining tests and strict all-target engine Clippy pass.
The release engine builds in 3m43s. These checks were insufficient: a later
reflection check catches a semantic regression before performance screening.

With TRUST_JS_TIER_THRESHOLD=0, a getter inside the small sloppy helper observes
h.f.caller===large as true on the pinned baseline and false on the prototype.
The tier log confirms publication of the larger caller. Narrowing growth
to strict functions alone is insufficient: the strict equivalent capturing
Error.stack prints get value, f, large on baseline, but only get value, large
on the prototype. The existing AST splice omits the logical callee frame.
Frame/lifetime recovery needs an explicit contract before admission expands;
merely retaining current allowlist guards does not preserve observations.
This report does not claim that smaller pre-existing inlining is unaffected.
